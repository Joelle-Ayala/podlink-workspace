## Positioning (one paragraph)
Podcast tools grade their own homework: your host counts your downloads,
then reports your downloads. Podlink reads from OP3 — open, independent,
checkable by the sponsor you're pitching — and now your assistant reads
the same numbers. Connect once, then ask: how did my show do, which apps
do my listeners use, what did I say in episode 12. Transcripts make it
more than a dashboard; the report makes it more than a toy.

## The 10 objections → answers (voice-guide §5 + personas, pre-drafted for
her 2-hour reply window; paste-speed on mobile)
1. "Another AI gimmick." → It's read-only plumbing to numbers you already
   own. The AI part is that you can finally ask instead of export.
2. "My host already has analytics." → Your host grades its own homework.
   OP3 is the independent counter a sponsor can check without you.
3. "Do I have to move hosts?" → No. Ever. It reads the RSS feed you
   already publish. We'd talk you out of migrating.
4. "Is my data safe?" → Read-only OAuth scoped to your own account; no
   tool can be asked about anyone else's show; revoke anytime.
5. "I'm not paying for Claude." → Fair — the connector needs Claude Pro.
   Podlink's dashboard shows the same numbers free, no assistant required.
6. "What does it cost?" → The connector and analytics reads are free on
   every plan. Transcribing an episode uses credits; you pick which ones.
7. "My show is small — is this for me?" → The median show does about 28
   downloads an episode. Checkable numbers matter MORE when you're small —
   they're how a small, specific audience gets sold.
8. "Downloads are a vanity metric." → Alone, yes. That's why the report
   weaves downloads with YouTube views, demographics and page clicks —
   the whole audience, not the shrinking half.
9. "Why should I trust you?" → You shouldn't have to — that's the point.
   The measurement source is open and independently operated. Check it.
10. "What's the catch?" → It only works on your own show. If you want
    market intel about other people's shows, that's not this.
