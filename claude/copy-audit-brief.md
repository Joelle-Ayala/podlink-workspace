# Copy Audit — Governing Brief (v1, 2026-08-20, Joelle's sharpened lens)
**Her framing, verbatim:** the site copy "is not really speaking to Podlink's USPs, not our
target audiences' and personas' common objections and pain points, and bridging that to our
main features and USPs." This brief is the audit's GOVERNING LENS, not a compliance layer.

## The three-beat test (every section of every page)
(a) **PAIN** — does it name the persona's actual pain in THEIR words (buyer-personas doc
    §0/§3)? The one-person podcast department · the four-hours-after-publish time sink ·
    the sponsor-credibility gap ("share trend data... to justify the increase").
(b) **OBJECTION** — does it meet their real objections head-on (inventory below)?
(c) **BRIDGE** — does it walk explicitly from that pain/objection to the specific Podlink
    USP that answers it? Feature description without the bridge = the drift, EVEN IF
    on-brand by tone. Flag it.
The after-copy must make the bridge VISIBLE in its structure: pain sentence → objection
acknowledged → USP as the answer, in that order or a tight variant.

## Objection inventory (SOURCED — mine, never invent)
**SaaS (S1/S2/S4):**
- AI slop / generic output — *"can become generic if you don't make templates that fit your
  branding"* (competitor churn review, personas §2-S2); *"The promise 'AI' in their name is
  only propaganda"* (S5 refund review). Answer: transcript-grounded + brand voice —
  "quotes what you actually said."
- "Another subscription" — S1 spends $1–25/mo TOTAL on tools. Answer: consolidation frame,
  now at v2 numbers (replaces ~$48 of link page + repurposer, plus what nobody sells).
- Switching costs / "do I have to move hosts" — answer: keep-your-host, RSS-only, no
  migration (already strong in FAQ; keep).
- "Will this sound like me" — same as slop objection; template/brand-voice ownership.
- "Can I trust the numbers" — answer: OP3 independence — *not our counter*, sponsor can
  check the source. (S4/P3's *"justify the increase"* verbatim.)
**Services (P1/P2/P3):**
- *"Which shows, specifically, could you actually book us on?"* (verbatim, lost deal) →
  named shows/placements strips.
- *"Is there an option to pay based on results?"* → pay-per-booking pricing up front
  ($750 + $449/booking answers it directly).
- *"Can we see as many case studies as you have available?"* → 17 case studies, linked.
- "We'll hire in-house" (P2's deferral pattern — MDS said it, came back, became flagship)
  → nurture copy, not argument: "when you're ready" tone.
- Tier confusion ("what does 'top industry' mean", "what is the fee for") → plain-language
  scope cards.
- Price/trust-an-agency → evidence-brief numbers + the honesty postures (attribution
  honesty IS a trust asset — say what we didn't do).

## USP register (the only approved bridge targets)
1. OP3 sponsor-defensible numbers — independent, checkable, free on every tier (no
   competitor gives this away).
2. Keep-your-host / no migration (RSS-only).
3. Episode-in → week-of-marketing-out, in YOUR voice (transcript-grounded, brand voice).
4. The hybrid: software AND a team — two doors, one vendor (personas §4.1 two-door split).
5. Template/brand-voice ownership — set your format once.
6. (When shipped, not before) the attribution loop — content → clicks → downloads; and the
   monetization-lockout wedge: "you don't need 20,000 downloads — you need the right
   audience, proven."
Claims-truth matrix governs tense: unshipped USPs may be bridged only future-tense/roadmap.
