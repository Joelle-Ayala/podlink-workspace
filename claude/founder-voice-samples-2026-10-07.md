## Sample F (first reply, qualifying)
"Thanks for following up! I'd love to hear more about the show and what
you're hoping to build. / What's the main metric you'd like to focus on:
audio streams, YouTube views, Instagram Reel views, or subscribers and
followers? You mentioned returning viewers too, so it would be helpful to
understand your top priority. / For context, I work with a podcast that's
now over 100,000 subscribers and just interviewed [major guest] this past
Sunday. We typically spend around $200 per episode on targeted YouTube ads
as one part of the broader growth strategy. … It's not a promise of the same
results, but depending on your goals, a focused YouTube test could be a
cost-effective place to start. / Happy to talk through the options, how we'd
measure success, and the management fee versus ad spend. Would [day] at
11 a.m. or [day] at 11 a.m. Eastern work for a 30-minute call? / Also, how
did you hear about us?"

"Great, [day] at 11 a.m. Eastern works. I'll send over the calendar invite.
/ We can focus the conversation on reaching viewers who are likely to watch

## Patterns to encode (voice-guide §register updates)
1. Opens warm + forward ("I'd love to hear more"), zero throat-clearing.
2. Qualifies with ONE concrete multiple-choice question, then asks the
   client to rank their own priority — she makes them define success.
3. Proof arrives as a casual operating detail, not a boast ("I work with a
   podcast that's now over 100,000 subscribers and just interviewed
   [guest]"), immediately followed by an honest hedge ("It's not a promise
   of the same results").
4. Prices appear as practice facts, not pitches ("$200 per episode …as one
   part of the broader strategy").
5. Fee transparency named head-on ("management fee versus ad spend") —
   never dodged.
6. Two concrete time slots offered, 30 minutes, timezone stated.
7. Confirmation emails MIRROR the client's own words back as the agenda
   ("viewers who are likely to watch and return" ← client's "people who
   actually watch the episodes and come back").
8. Sign-off: plain "Best, Joelle" / "Looking forward" — no flourish.
