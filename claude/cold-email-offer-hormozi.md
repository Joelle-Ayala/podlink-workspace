## 1. The diagnosis Hormozi would open with

He would not let you sell the $19/mo SaaS on cold email. Not because the product
is weak — because the arithmetic is fatal.

His governing constraint from *$100M Leads* is **LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1**, and his
channel rule is blunt: *outbound is the most expensive lead source per lead, so
it only works when each customer is worth a lot.*

| | Pro at $19/mo direct | 90-day service container |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $19/mo | $7,200 (3 × $2,400) |
| Gross margin | ~70% (AI + Whisper + Stripe) | ~55–65% (labor-loaded) |
| Realistic retention | 8–14 months | + rev-share annuity + continuity |
| **Lifetime gross profit** | **~$110–190** | **~$4,000–4,700 before back end** |
| CAC cold email can afford | **< $60** | **< $1,500** |

You cannot originate, personalize, send, reply-handle, book, show, and close a
human being for under $60. So Hormozi's answer is not "make the SaaS pitch
better." It is:

> **Cold email sells the high-ticket outcome. The SaaS is the delivery
> mechanism, the free gift, and the back end — it is never the thing you ask a
> stranger to buy.**

This is his standard structure, just applied to software: *sell the result, give
away the tool.* The SaaS's job on this channel is three things — (1) the free
personalized artifact that earns the reply, (2) a stacked bonus that inflates
offer value at ~zero marginal cost, (3) the continuity that keeps paying after
the service engagement ends.

Your own memory of the business already agrees: retainer target is
**$3,000–$7,000/mo**. That is the number cold email is built to hit. $19 is not.

## 2. Audience: the starving-crowd screen

Hormozi picks a market on four criteria — **massive pain, purchasing power, easy
to target, growing.** Scored against your actual personas:

| Persona | Pain | Purchasing power | Targetable | Growing | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| P4 — Agency reseller | — | — | — | — | Inbound only, thin margin |

**The pick: expert-led shows where the podcast feeds a business.**

Two reasons Hormozi would land here and nowhere else:

1. **You can diagnose them from public data.** A public RSS feed tells you
   cadence, episode count, whether the show notes are three sentences, whether
   there are sponsors, whether the link page is a Linktree, whether they're on
   YouTube. Every other persona requires guessing. Hormozi's cold-outreach
   doctrine is *big fast value delivered before the ask* — a public feed is the
   only one of your personas that lets you manufacture that value at scale, for
   free, with the tool you already built.
2. **The proof is already in the book.** Every multi-renewal client you have is
   this persona. The flagship went from small engagement to ~$112K lifetime.
   That's the highest-likelihood-of-achievement evidence you own.

### The exact list definition (write this into the query)

Include a show if **all** of these are true:
- Publishing **≥ 2 episodes/month for the last 90 days**, and **≥ 12 months old**
- **Solo or duo host** whose name/business is the show (coach, consultant,
  author, practitioner, agency owner) — the About page sells something
- A **business URL in the feed** that offers a service, program, or course
- **No sponsor read** detectable in the last 5 episode descriptions
- **At least one of:** show notes under ~150 words · no clips/short-form on the

Exclude: network-owned shows, top-100 charting shows, anything under 6 episodes,
enterprise-branded shows (that's the S4 test list, different sequence).

**Where the list comes from:** Podcast Index + RSS + site crawl — exactly
Phase 1 of `contact-discovery-spec.md`. No vendor, no gate, no Snov account
needed, no legal question to resolve. You dogfood your own product to build the
list you sell it with, which is also the best origin story you'll ever have on a
sales call.

**Burn the 12,983-contact list.** It is VC-backed enterprise CMOs and it is the

list problem misdiagnosed as a channel problem, and would not spend an hour

## 3. The lead magnet (the thing that actually earns the reply)

Hormozi's lead-magnet rule: **solve a narrow problem completely, and reveal the
bigger problem you get paid to solve.** Give away the *what*, sell the *how*.

### "The Show Report" — free, personalized, generated from their public feed

One page, their name and cover art on it, built from data that costs you nothing:

1. **Downloads + trend** (OP3, read live — already free in your product)
2. **Publishing consistency** vs. their category
3. **Post-publish audit** — show-notes length, clip presence, link-page state,
   YouTube presence, subscribe-link health
4. **The Sponsorable Score** — where their show sits against the industry's
   10k–20k downloads-per-episode gate, and what audience quality can be proven
   *instead* of raw volume
5. **The gap line:** the specific missing piece, e.g. *"Nothing you publish is
   tracked, so you cannot prove which promotion moved downloads — and no sponsor
   will pay without that."*

This is the sharpest weapon you have because it is simultaneously the lead
magnet, the demo, the diagnosis, the sales script, and the reason they open a
free Podlink account. The report *is* your product's episode-report concept,
shipped as marketing before it ships as software.

**Hormozi's stack rank of lead magnets applies exactly:** a personalized
assessment outperforms a guide, because it makes the prospect the subject.

## 4. The Grand Slam Offer

### 4.1 Name it (his MAGIC formula)

> **The 90-Day Podcast Payback Plan**
> *For expert-led shows: you record, we run everything after publish — and in 90
> days you'll know exactly what's working, or you'll be sponsorable, or you
> don't pay.*

- **M**agnetic reason why: *"You've published 80 episodes and you still can't
  answer what the show is worth."*
- **A**vatar: expert-led shows, 500–10,000 downloads/episode
- **G**oal: payback — proof the show earns, plus the first path to it paying
- **I**nterval: 90 days
- **C**ontainer: "Plan" / "Sprint"

### 4.2 The problem→solution stack

Hormozi builds the offer by listing every obstacle between the prospect and the
dream outcome, then assigning a deliverable to each. Nothing here requires
features you haven't built or scoped:

| Their obstacle (verbatim-adjacent) | What's in the box | Source in the spec |
|---|---|---|
| "The 2–4 hours after every episode" | **Episode Content Kit, done for you** — transcript, show notes, newsletter, 8–10 social posts, all quoting what they actually said | Pro tier, build order step 2 |
| "It sounds like generic AI, not like me" | **Brand voice auto-populated from their own feed**, transcript-grounded generation | Spec §2 Layer 1–2 |
| "I don't know if any of it works" | **Tracked links in every generated post** + per-episode destination pages + the monthly correlation view | Spec §5, gaps 1/2/4 |
| "My link page is a dead end" | **Bio link rebuild** with per-episode pages and **subscribe deep-links** (tap → Apple/Spotify → follow) | Spec §5 gap 3 — cheap, free-tier |
| "Half my audience is on YouTube and I can't see it" | **YouTube connected**, per-episode views merged into one report | Build order step 2, YouTube Lite |
| "I have 120 old episodes doing nothing" | **Back-catalog revival — 12 evergreen kits** from the archive | Existing capability |
| "No agency will talk to me under 20k downloads" | **Sponsorable Media Kit + rate card + your sponsorship desk at 20% rev share, no setup fee** | Decision sheet, adopted 2026-08-19 |
| "I don't have time to learn another tool" | **Done-for-you for 90 days**, then it's a 20-minute-a-week habit | Service + SaaS handoff |

### 4.3 Price

| | Offer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **$2,400/mo, 3-month minimum ($7,200)** | Inside your stated $3–7K retainer band; above the $950 editing bundle + $750 growth because the container includes proof + sponsor path |
| **Paid in full** | **$5,997** | Hormozi: never discount the offer, discount the *terms*. PIF saves them $1,203 and fixes your cash flow |
| **Downsell (only if they say no twice)** | **$1,500/mo, 3 months** — kit + bio link + report, no back-catalog, no clips, no sponsor desk | Protects the anchor |
| **Back end (the real money)** | Sponsorship at **20% rev share** + **Pro/Creator continuity** after month 3 | The annuity |

Hormozi's line: *if nobody is complaining about your price, it's too low.* At
$7,200 against a $112K flagship LTV, you are still underpricing the ceiling.

### 4.4 The guarantee stack — build this on things you control

This is where most offers die. **Never guarantee downloads.** You don't control
them, and the standing evidence rules forbid claims you can't source.

| Type | The guarantee | Why it's safe |
|---|---|---|
| **Conditional / performance (headline)** | *"In 90 days you'll have 12 episode kits published, 12 tracked episode pages live, and a monthly report showing which post drove which clicks — or we work free until you do."* | 100% within your delivery control. Deadline-based, not outcome-based. |
| **Anti-guarantee (the magnet)** | *"We don't charge a setup fee to sell your sponsorships. We take 20% of what we sell. If we sell nothing, we make nothing."* | Already your adopted rate card. Answers the #2 verbatim objection: *"Is there an option to pay based on results?"* |
| **Unconditional** | *"30 days. If you don't like the first month's work, refund — and every kit, transcript, and page we built stays yours."* | Marginal cost is near-zero; the psychological lift is enormous |
| **Implied / partnership** | Rev share on sponsorship — you only win when they do | Structural, not promised |

Do **not** offer "double your downloads or your money back." It is the most
tempting line available and the one that will bankrupt the delivery team.

### 4.5 Bonuses (Hormozi: bonuses beat discounts, and each gets a stated value)

Present these as an itemized stack after the price, never before:

1. **Podlink Pro, free for life while you're a client** — $228/yr *(near-zero
   marginal cost, and it's the continuity seed)*
2. **The Sponsorable Media Kit** — verified audience data pack a brand can
   defend internally — $1,500
3. **Back-catalog revival: 12 evergreen kits** from old episodes — $1,200
4. **One booked guest appearance on another show** — $449 *(your existing
   pay-per-booking rate; also a live demo of the booking service)*
5. **Subscribe deep-links + per-episode pages**, yours permanently — $600
6. **Direct line to the sponsorship desk** the day you cross the threshold — $0
   fee, 20% rev share

Stated stack value: **~$3,977 in bonuses on a $7,200 engagement**, before the
core deliverables.

### 4.6 Scarcity & urgency — make it real, because it protects delivery

Your own data shows both documented churn events were **service lapses during
capacity gaps**. So the honest constraint is also the correct one:

> **"We onboard 6 shows a month. Not a marketing line — the two clients we lost
> in five years, we lost because we took on more than we could serve. When the
> 6 are full, the next start date is the following month."**

Hormozi's rule is that scarcity must be true or it poisons the brand. This one
is true, it's flattering to the buyer, and it doubles as a trust signal.

## 5. The cold email system

### 5.1 Sequence architecture

Five emails, plain text, one idea each, **one CTA, always a reply — never a
link** in emails 1–2. Hormozi's ask escalation: *give value → ask permission →
ask for the call.*

**Email 1 — the free gift, permission-based (Day 0)**

> Subject: `your show`
>
> Hi {First} — I ran {Show Name} through our tool this morning.
>
> {One specific, true observation from the feed: "you've published 84 episodes,
> 3x a month since {Month Year}, and your show notes average 41 words."}
>
> It spit out a one-page report — downloads trend, what your post-publish
> workflow is leaving on the table, and where you sit against what sponsors
> actually require.
>
> Want me to send it? Just reply "send it" and it's yours. No call, no pitch.
>
> — Joelle

**Email 2 — deliver, then the gap (Day 3, or immediately on reply)**

Attach/paste the report. Close with the one line that creates the next problem:

> The thing the report can't tell you: which of your posts actually moved
> downloads. Nobody's tool joins those two numbers today — that's the piece we
> build for clients.

**Email 3 — proof, no ask (Day 7)**

One case study, one persona-matched, told in three sentences. Format:
*where they started → what we ran → what happened.* Then: *"Happy to send the
full write-up if it's useful — reply 'send' and I will."*

**Email 4 — the offer, stated plainly (Day 12)**

> We do this as a 90-day thing: you record, we run everything after publish —
> kits, tracked pages, the report, your link page, YouTube — and if you're not
> where we said you'd be in 90 days we work free until you are. Sponsorship is
> rev-share only, no fee.
>
> Worth 15 minutes?

**Email 5 — the close-the-file (Day 20)**

> Should I close your file? No hard feelings either way — reply "not now" and
> I'll stop. If it's just timing, tell me the month and I'll come back then.

Hormozi's highest-reply email in every deployment is some version of #5.

### 5.2 Deliverability & infra (non-negotiable before send #1)

- **Separate sending domains** — `getpodlink.com`, `podlinkhq.com`. Never send
  cold from `podlink.ai`; you cannot afford to burn the domain the app lives on.
- 3–5 inboxes per domain, **14–21 days of warm-up**, **≤ 30 sends/inbox/day**
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC on every sending domain
- No images, no tracking pixels, no links in email 1, no HTML signature
- Physical address + one-line opt-out in the footer (CAN-SPAM)
- **This is not blocked by the OAuth verification item in the handoff.** That
  gates the *in-product* outreach feature. Your own sending stack is independent
  and can start this week.

### 5.3 Personalization at scale

Hormozi's standard is *"the first line must be impossible to have written for
anyone else."* You are unusually well-positioned: the RSS feed hands you episode
count, cadence, latest title, show-notes length, sponsor presence, and link-page
state. One templated sentence assembled from real feed data clears the bar and
scales to thousands. **Do not use "I loved your episode about X."** Everyone
does it and everyone knows.

## 7. The ascension ladder (where the SaaS actually gets sold)

```
Cold email
  → Free Show Report                       (no login, no friction)
    → Free Podlink account                 (analytics + bio link = the loop's endpoints)
      → 90-Day Payback Plan   $7,200       (the cold-email offer)
        → Sponsorship rev share 20%        (annuity; the anti-guarantee pays off)
        → Pro / Creator  $19–49/mo         (continuity, forever)
          → Referral                       (the channel that actually built the book)
```

Hormozi's insight applied to your specific product: **the SaaS is not the sale,
it's the switching cost.** By month three the client's transcripts, link page,
tracked episode pages, sponsor data, and brand voice all live in Podlink.
Cancelling the service is easy. Cancelling the account means their audience's
destination goes dark. That's why you give Pro away free during the engagement —
it costs you almost nothing and it makes the $19 renewal the most obvious
decision they'll make that year.

**And the referral step is not decoration.** Your own data says the winners came
from referrals and inbound. Hormozi's rule — every delivered client is asked, at
the moment of peak result, for two names — should be in the month-3 offboarding
script, not left to chance.

## 9. What Hormozi would explicitly tell you NOT to do

1. **Don't cold email the $19 tier.** Covered. It's the whole point of §1.
2. **Don't revive the 12,983-contact list.** 215 documented ghosts. New list.
3. **Don't lead with the software.** Lead with the outcome. The tool is a
   *because*, not a *what*.
4. **Don't guarantee downloads or revenue.** Guarantee your own deliverables and
   deadlines. Use rev-share for the outcome-linked piece.
5. **Don't discount the price.** Add bonuses or change the terms. Never the
   number.
6. **Don't run two offers at once until one is boring.** One avatar, one offer.
7. **Don't sell the attribution loop as shipped.** It's build-order step 3. Sell
   *the report* and *tracked links* only as far as they're actually live — see
   §10.
8. **Don't outrun delivery.** Six a month. The two churn events in your history
   were capacity failures, and Hormozi's own most-repeated regret is scaling
   sales past fulfillment.
