# Podlink — Buyer Personas, Messaging & Positioning

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Basis:** HubSpot segmentation of all 297 deals (77 won / 215 ghosted-lost),
voice-of-customer mining of ~20 real buying threads in Gmail, the 12,983-contact
prospect database, and market research across competitor reviews (Capterra, app
stores, HN), podcaster population stats, and 2026 tool pricing. Verbatim quotes
are real and sourced; nothing is invented.
**Use:** the source of truth for website copy, ad messaging, and content.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph summary

The data says Podlink has **two businesses with one center of gravity**. Services
sold best to founder-led small companies and solo experts — never to the
enterprise CMOs the cold lists targeted, who ghosted at $7,500 almost every
time. The SaaS market's highest-value segments are the same people ten minutes
earlier in their journey: experts whose show feeds their business, and the
marketers who run company shows. Positioning that works for both: **Podlink is
the promotion and audience layer — we handle everything after you hit publish,
and we can prove what worked.** Sell time to the solo host, voice to the expert,
proof to the marketer, and audiences to the sponsor.

## 1. Services-side personas (from actual buyers)

### P1 — The Visibility Founder
**Who:** founder/CEO or hands-on marketing director at a founder-led B2B tech
company, 10–250 employees (crypto, SaaS, fintech-adjacent, insights). The
buyers: "Founder & CEO," "Head of Marketing," "Director of Integrated Media,"
"Chief Marketing Officer." Examples: Mudrex, QualSights, ShiftPixy, T3 Live,
Opolis, Koii.
**What they buy:** guest booking + clips, the $6,350–$13,350 packages.
**Their words:** *"grow name recognition and establish thought leadership"* ·
*"if these efforts prove to be fruitful"* · *"worthwhile interview
opportunities."*
**Trigger:** internal exec buy-in ("Our COO is on board"), funding, a launch, or
a founder who simply decides — the fastest close signed and paid within 6 days
of the proposal.
**Objections (verbatim from real threads):**
1. *"Which shows, specifically, could you actually book us on?"*
2. *"Is there an option to pay based on results?"*
3. *"Can we see as many case studies as you have available?"*
4. Tier confusion — "what does 'top industry' mean," "what is the $350 fee for."
**Messaging that fits:** named shows, named placements, pay-per-booking pricing
front and center (we HAVE it: $650 + $399/booking — it directly answers
objection 2), and the 17 case studies (objection 3). Lead with "lead generation
and content marketing in one motion," their outcome language.
**Anti-pattern proven by data:** this persona at enterprise scale (1,000+
employees) does not close from cold outreach — Aptean, PAR, ControlUp, Dave,
Protocol Labs all ghosted. Healthcare and banking never closed once. Don't
spend against them.

### P2 — The Expert Who Is the Business
**Who:** solo expert, coach, author, practitioner — founder-titled, strongly
female-skewed in the actual client base (divorce, motherhood, wellness,
coaching, brand strategy). Examples: My Divorce Solution, Michelle
Dempsey-Multack, Maria Ross, Caroline Fornaris, Abbey Williams, Amanda Paz.
**What they buy:** production + clips + growth — starting small ($1,125–$3,600)
and renewing into the largest lifetime values in the book (MDS ≈ $112K). Every
multi-renewal client is this persona. They treat the vendor as **their entire
marketing department**.
**Their words:** they arrive with a show and a burden, not a media strategy —
*"just trying to get everything on paper so I can start planning."*
**The signature pattern:** the "we'll hire in-house" objection is deferral, not
rejection — MDS said exactly that, came back within a month, and became the
flagship. Nurture, don't argue.
**Messaging that fits:** "you record, we do everything else" (the editing page
already leads with this) · the MMO story (4 renewals → we sold her a 17-month
sponsorship) shows the full arc this persona wants: burden → growth → money.
**Risk to manage:** this persona churns on *attention*, not results — both
documented complaints (KMG, Madam Trader) were service lapses during capacity
gaps. Capacity before demand.

### P3 — The Niche-Audience Sponsor
**Who:** brand-side marketer buying access to a creator's audience — "Sr.
Director, Strategic Initiatives" (Worthy), "Director, Employer Branding" (CVS),
media agencies (Ad Results for Ritual).
**What they buy:** host-read sponsorships on shows we represent; renewals when
the numbers hold.
**Their words:** *"share any trend data such as downloads per month... to model
and justify the increase."* This buyer must justify internally — they buy
evidence, not vibes.
**Messaging that fits:** the sponsorship page's honesty about attribution is
exactly right for them; audience data packs and tracked links are the product.
The Ritual case study (indie show sold at national-brand rates, same flight as
Theo Von) speaks directly to the agency version of this buyer.

### P4 — The Agency Reseller (channel, not persona)
KMG generated 6+ engagements inbound — but at $412–$850 wholesale per booking.
Real demand signal, thin economics. Keep the white-label rate card, don't build
messaging around it.

## 2. SaaS-side personas (from market evidence)

### S1 — The 28-Download Solo Host *(Free tier's job)*
52% of podcasters do everything themselves; the median show does ~28
downloads/episode; 72% name growth as problem #1 and the modal growth strategy
is "I don't have one." Spends $1–25/month on ALL tools.
**JTBD:** make the post-publish chore disappear; finally see if anything works.
**Verbatim market pain:** *"content creators spend more time writing
descriptions, show notes, and social posts than actually creating content."*
**Messaging:** free analytics + free link page = "see your show clearly, free."
$19 is sold as **consolidation** — it replaces Linktree/Podpage ($9–15) plus a
repurposer plus a headline tool, not as another add-on.
**Also:** this is the store funnel's identity buyer (plaque, flag, light box).

### S2 — The Expert-Entrepreneur *(Pro's core buyer)*
Coach/consultant/course-creator whose show feeds the business — the SaaS twin
of services P2, and the persona competitor review rosters are actually full of.
**JTBD:** "turn every episode into a week of marketing *in my voice*."
**The sensitivity:** generic output. Competitor churn is documented on exactly
this — *"can become generic if you don't make templates that fit your
branding."* Podlink's transcript-grounded, brand-voice-from-your-feed generation
is the direct answer; say it that way: **"quotes what you actually said."**
**Pricing:** $19 is trivial against VA hours ("saved me 100's of hours" energy).

### S3 — The Fractional Producer / Podcast VA / Small Agency *(future tier)*
21% of podcasters hire help; every competitor ships an Agency tier. Manages
2–10 shows, needs per-client delivery at margin. Not a launch persona — note
for the roadmap that "1 show per user" is currently hardcoded, which blocks
this entire segment until changed.

### S4 — The Company Show Marketer *(the attribution loop's buyer)*
Runs a B2B thought-leadership show; 76% of business podcasts exist for thought
leadership. **JTBD: "prove the podcast is working to my boss."** No tool on the
market closes content → clicks → downloads today — this is the white space the
positioning spec claims, and this persona is who pays for it. Also the SaaS
twin of services P1: same company, doing it themselves.
**Credibility ammo:** research found NO measured time savings from existing AI
podcast tools (Alitu study) — the market's promises are soft; an attribution
loop is hard evidence.

### S5 — The Video-First Clipper *(Creator tier, post-launch)*
31–51% of shows now do video; OpusClip's base skews marketer; churn documented
on credit waste and generic clips (*"The promise 'AI' in their name is only
propaganda"* — a refund-and-switch review). Clip Studio's pitch when it comes:
clip selection informed by what your analytics say resonates — not another
credit-burning generic clipper.

### The monetization lockout (two-sided wedge)
*"Most podcast advertising agencies won't work with you until you have 10k–20k
downloads PER episode"* — vs. a median show at ~28. Podlink's sponsorship
service + verified data serves exactly the shows the industry locks out. This
is a message no competitor can copy: **"you don't need 20,000 downloads to be
sponsorable — you need the right audience, proven."**

## 3. Positioning (the sentences to build everything on)

**Master positioning:** *Podlink is the promotion and audience layer of the
podcast stack. Record and edit wherever you like — we handle everything after
publish, and we prove what worked.*

**Services line:** *The team behind the growth — production, clips, booking,
sponsorship and growth for people who'd rather run their business than their
post-production.*

**Per-persona value props:**
| Persona | Buy | The sentence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Visibility Founder | Booking + clips | "Lead generation and content marketing in one motion — here are the shows, here are the case studies, and you can pay per booking." |
| P2 Expert | Production + growth | "You record. We do everything else — and when the audience is big enough, we sell the ads on it." |
| P3 Sponsor | Sponsorship | "Audiences you can't reach on the top 100, with data you can defend internally." |
| S1 Solo Host | Free → $19 | "See your show clearly, free. Then replace three tools with one for $19." |
| S2 Expert-Entrepreneur | Pro $19 | "A week of marketing from every episode — in your voice, quoting what you actually said." |
| S4 Company Marketer | Pro + loop | "The first report that shows which post drove which downloads. Prove the podcast works." |

**Words to use** (theirs): thought leadership · name recognition · worthwhile ·
prove · in my voice · downloads per month · which shows specifically.
**Words to avoid:** synergy-speak, "revolutionary AI," reach multipliers,
anything the CVS episode taught us not to say.

## 4. What this changes on the website

1. **Homepage:** currently product-only. Needs the two-door split above the
   fold: "Software that handles post-publish" / "A team that does it for you" —
   the data shows these are different buyers at different moments, and the nav
   already separates them; the homepage should too.
2. **Booking page:** promote pay-per-booking pricing higher (it answers the #1
   and #2 real objections) and add a named-shows strip — "which shows,
   specifically" was asked verbatim by a lost deal.
3. **Pricing page:** the $19 tier's frame should be consolidation ("replaces
   your link page + repurposer + headline tool"), per S1's spend ceiling.
4. **Sponsorship page:** add the monetization-lockout message — "you don't need
   20,000 downloads." It's the sharpest differentiated line in the research.
5. **Case-studies index:** group by persona journey, not just service — P1
   buyers asked for case studies by name; there are now 17.
6. **MCP page:** S2/S4 are its audiences; current copy already fits.

## 5. Content pillars (one per persona, from their own questions)

1. "Which shows should I pitch?" — booking-side content for P1 (show lists per
   vertical; the pitch template lead magnet already exists).
2. The post-publish workload — S1/S2 (the benchmark tool, "the 4 hours after
   every episode" content).
3. Prove-it content — S4/P3 (attribution loop, episode reports, "podcast ROI").
4. Monetization for normal-sized shows — the lockout wedge (rate-card guides,
   "sponsorable at 500 downloads").
5. The growth-system story — Go With Elmo as the flagship narrative (31M views,
   the audio-to-video shift).

## 6. Cautions carried over from the data

- The Worthy relationship **ended with the client terminating after two months
  of silence in 2024**. The 17-month case study is factual, but get Worthy's
  blessing before featuring them heavily, and never claim an ongoing
  relationship.
- The 12,983-contact list is the ghost machine — VC-backed enterprise CMOs.
  Don't revive it; the winners came from referrals and inbound.
- ERI/Shegerian and Mevi are excluded from case studies (documented
  dissatisfaction; no delivered placements, respectively). Education Modified
  is real but not podcast work, and its 48% figure has no primary source.
- ETHDenver was never a client (SporkDAO membership + attendance only).
