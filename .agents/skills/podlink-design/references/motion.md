# Motion

Motion is a tool for explaining state, hierarchy or story. It is not evidence of premium design.

## Product UI

Good motion:

- confirming a state change,
- expanding/collapsing detail,
- showing loading/progress honestly,
- connecting an action to its result,
- subtle spatial continuity between related views.

Product motion should be fast, restrained and interruptible.

Avoid cinematic scrolling, ambient movement and delayed controls inside operational screens.

## Marketing

Major acquisition pages may use richer motion when it supports the page grammar.

Good examples:

- one episode connecting to audio + YouTube + derivative assets,
- a report assembling from real sources,
- a before/after state reveal,
- controlled depth in a hero when the depth communicates the system.

## Signature interaction

At most one primary signature interaction on a major page unless the page concept explicitly requires more.

It should be describable in product terms, not animation terms.

Good:

> As you scroll, one episode resolves into its connected audiences and assets.

Weak:

> The cards parallax at different speeds.

## ScrollCraft principles to borrow

Useful concepts:

- choose a page grammar first,
- plan an emotion/energy curve,
- create one visual peak,
- use separate mobile art direction,
- avoid repeating the same motion device,
- verify real scroll states visually.

Do not assume ScrollCraft's engine, generated worlds or long cinematic pages are appropriate for Podlink.

## Reduced motion

Every motion-heavy concept must have a reduced-motion design before shipping.

Reduced motion must preserve:

- content,
- ordering,
- hierarchy,
- primary action,
- meaning of the signature interaction.

Do not merely set duration to zero if the layout depended on animation to reveal required content.

## Performance

Prefer transforms/opacity for decorative transition when appropriate.

Do not ship expensive animation that competes with real screenshots/video or degrades Core Web Vitals for a marginal visual effect.

Product video/screen recording often communicates a workflow more honestly and cheaply than complex scroll choreography.
